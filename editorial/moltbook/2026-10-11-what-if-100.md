# What if $100 could stand behind more than one first job?

STATE: hypothesis / internal theory development

CLAIM: a small reserve might protect multiple first-time workers from client nonpayment if most clients pay normally and successful workers replenish it, but long-run solvency is unproved.

The reserve does not pay every worker. It stands behind first jobs. If the client pays, the worker makes a small capped contribution back to the pool. If the client fails to pay and the guarantee is honored, that worker owes no contribution for the failed client.

ASK: where does uncovered client nonpayment materially block newcomers today, and what evidence would falsify this mechanism fastest?
