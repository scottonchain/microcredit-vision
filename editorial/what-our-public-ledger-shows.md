# What our public ledger shows, and what it cannot

Anyone can open a public ledger of our practice lending pool. This post, written by Claude Code, an AI agent, explains it for readers who are not engineers. The ledger is [here](https://scottonchain.github.io/microcredit-agent-testbed/dashboard/).

**It is a closed test.** Every dollar in it is a test dollar on a public test network, and nothing in it can pay a bill. The ledger does not establish that any borrower was a real person.

**What it records.** Lenders put test dollars into one pool. Borrowers draw small loans and repay them. In the latest saved snapshot, dated 4 November 2026 00:08 UTC, 128 test dollars were deposited, 45 were lent, and 45.00152 came back. Twenty-seven loans were repaid on time, one was cancelled, and none defaulted. Those are the test pool's own books. That the pool counts correctly says nothing about whether real people would repay.

**Where interest goes.** The first reading of the ledger treated all interest as lender gain. It is not. The 0.00152 test dollars of interest paid was split: 0.000684 went to a protective reserve and 0.000836 raised lenders' value. The corrected ledger shows gross interest, fees, the reserve and lenders' value as separate lines.

**What it does not measure.** It does not show how many real people were helped, what sponsors spent, or whether anyone earned more. Those lines read "not measured", and no real-person profiles are collected or shown.

**How fresh it is.** The saved snapshot is labelled historical and not re-verified. The corrected ledger is reviewed but not live, and the page you can open today still carries the older labels. A refresh is scheduled once a day; if a refresh fails, the page keeps the last good data and says so. Treat the dated figures, not the page's headline wording, as the record.

**What a contributor can conclude.** The practice pool's accounting worked as designed in a small test. It cannot support a claim about repayment, poverty or sponsor value.
