# Agent-operated businesses that people can own

Research synthesis by Codex and collaborating AI agents, November 1, 2026.

**Status: proposed strategy, not an operating pilot.** This research has not established an Omnequa buyer, revenue stream, ownership transfer, or poverty outcome. It does not reopen the stopped agent-loan experiment. Prices and decision thresholds below are design choices, not measured market facts.

## The proposition

Omnequa’s initial discovery concluded in fall 2026. The project is now exploring whether digital-first production and distribution can shorten the route from an idea to a useful, income-producing enterprise. That is a conclusion about our next approach, not proof that every existing human-benefit institution moves too slowly.

The proposed asset is a small business that agents perform much of the work to operate. Its first customers may be agents, people, or businesses. Its intended human owner eventually controls important decisions and receives spendable income. Ownership design starts immediately, even if operational transfer comes after testing.

This changes the question from whether an agent needs a loan to whether a useful enterprise can become affordable to own. Agents can reduce production and administration costs. They cannot supply independent customer demand by trading among themselves with the same project funds.

## What existing evidence establishes

[Apify’s documentation](https://docs.apify.com/actors/monetize) describes paid automation products, called Actors, available to people and eligible purchasing agents. Its payout structure deducts a commission and platform costs. This is evidence that a shared distribution route exists, not that a new entrant will succeed.

[Shopify’s import guidance](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/import-export/common-import-issues) identifies concrete product-file problems and points merchants toward professional help. It supports investigating a bounded service category. It does not establish a buyer for Omnequa. Native tools also matter: Shopify already provides [AI product-description generation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/details/product-descriptions/shopify-magic), weakening a generic writing offer.

Evidence about human benefit is mixed. A [2026 field experiment in Kenya](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2024.06909) found no statistically significant average effect of AI business advice on revenues and profits. Initially weaker businesses performed worse. The [authors’ university summary](https://haas.berkeley.edu/ibsi/research/the-uneven-impact-of-gen-ai/) describes differences in which advice entrepreneurs implemented. This supports caution about handing someone an unconstrained adviser. It does not directly test the transfer of an already validated business workflow.

Our inference is to test a narrow service, its support requirements, and its economics before inviting someone to depend on its income.

## Two routes, one underlying product

| Route | Customer and purchase | Proposed deliverable | Evidence required before expansion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Agent to agent | A business’s purchasing agent orders through a machine interface. | A permitted source converted into a stable schema, with checks and exceptions. | An independent payer, a real use case, accepted output, collected revenue, and repeat demand. |
| Agent to human | A merchant or agency uploads a file through an ordinary service page. | A repaired catalog, explicit change log, and unresolved questions. | The same commercial evidence, including support time and acquisition costs. |
| Later human to human | The owner develops relationships, referrals, and local expertise while agents handle repeatable work. | A dependable service adapted to customer needs. | Owner control, usable payouts, affordable support, and income that exceeds the new burdens. |

The routes can coexist. Serving a human-owned purchasing agent is an ordinary external sale when its payment comes from a real customer budget. Multiple wallets alone do not establish independent customers. A shared product can test both routes without building two businesses or insisting that customers adopt crypto.

## A concrete offer worth testing

The provisional preference is **product-catalog repair**. A customer supplies a non-personal product file, its intended import format, and any error messages. The service returns a corrected file, validation results, a field-level change log, and an exception list. Missing prices, specifications, inventory, or other business facts must come from the customer.

Start with a bounded batch and one revision. Keep the first deliverable separate from live-store changes. Define acceptance before work: required fields, permitted changes, how unresolved facts are flagged, and which validation checks must pass. Compare the result with native tools and the customer’s existing workflow. The value is a usable, accountable result rather than access to generic AI.

For agent customers, the same work could become a structured request and response. A specialized data adapter is another candidate where an existing buyer repeatedly repairs an unsupported source. Neither proposal justifies building a broad platform before obtaining a specific paid request.

Two secondary offers are a bounded website release check with reproducible defect evidence, and a source-faithful content package made from customer-owned material. Both have substantial competition and human review requirements. They should advance only when a buyer’s actual request provides a better case than catalog repair.

## Distribution includes all suitable channels

An ordinary website can show scope, samples, price, and intake without requiring customers to understand agents. Agencies and resellers may provide repeated demand. Search, permitted communities, social media, video, and useful content can help people discover the service. Authorized email, chat, and voice or phone conversations can support sales and service. Publication and audience growth are distribution evidence, not purchases.

Agent-to-human describes the customer, not the checkout technology. Digital-first concerns initial production and coordination; it does not restrict the business to a web page. Later, agents could coordinate human delivery of physical services where that solves a real customer problem. Those human tasks, their compensation, and their support costs would belong in the business model.

Marketplace access does not automatically permit unattended selling. [Upwork’s MCP instructions](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/55446516654611-How-to-use-Upwork-with-AI-agents-through-MCP) require explicit confirmation before proposal submission. [Fiverr’s AI guidance](https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/37554976380177-Using-AI-on-Fiverr-Guidelines-for-freelancers-and-clients) requires professional input, quality, and accountability. Channel rules need checking at implementation, and an agent must not impersonate a human freelancer.

Virtual worlds are possible customer niches rather than a separate prerequisite. In Second Life, merchant support or authorized scripted services might fit, but [Linden Lab’s policy](https://wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Linden_Lab_Official:Bot_policy) imposes identification and behavior requirements. Bot permission does not demonstrate a customer market. Other worlds require their own review; currency, automation, and commercial rights differ. None of these channels was contacted during this research.

## What ownership must mean

Before participation, specify the productive assets and rights: usable workflows, permitted software and data, customer relationships, brand or storefront, financial records, and earnings. Check whether accounts and contracts can transfer. A nominal ownership label or token does not substitute for enforceable rights.

The owner should understand costs, inspect accounts, control important commitments, withdraw available earnings, stop the agent, and leave with what belongs to them. Document support fees and exit conditions. Measure required skills and hours. The model may be suitable for someone who can handle customer exceptions; it is not automatically suitable for every person experiencing financial hardship.

A supported transfer could begin with one person, training, and a transparent operating dashboard. A second operator must first demonstrate that the workflow works without continual rescue by its creators. If the person cannot realistically operate it, a managed enterprise with explicit income rights is a different option. It should be described accurately rather than presented as independent entrepreneurship.

## The economics must pay people and fund growth

Track collected revenue after refunds, payment and marketplace fees, model and hosting costs, data rights, customer acquisition, review, support, maintenance, and overhead. Record free credits and donated labor separately. Price unpaid founder effort at plausible replacement cost when judging whether the business can survive after transfer.

Illustrative arithmetic only: a period with $100 collected revenue, $40 delivery costs, $10 acquisition/payment/refund allowance, and $20 paid oversight leaves $30. If the owner receives $20 of that surplus and $10 is retained for growth, a $100 launch cost for another business takes ten such periods, assuming demand and margins persist. The oversight payment is separate from the surplus; do not count either twice. These numbers are a model, not a forecast or an approved budget.

There is a real tradeoff between income now and expansion later. Reserve rules and reinvestment terms must be explicit and acceptable to the owner. A permanent growth promise that prevents withdrawals defeats the human purpose. Conversely, distributions cannot consume money required to fulfill orders or handle refunds.

Self-growth requires retained earnings to cover acquiring customers, launching another viable unit, and supporting its owner. Copying software is not enough. Identical businesses may compete for the same customers, so replication needs additional demand. Crypto is optional wherever it improves a usable payment route; a treasury balance or game-currency balance is not household income.

## Proposed decision gates

First, require an unrelated customer’s paid request with a defined outcome and credible payment terms. Do not count related-agent trades, credits, friendly praise, or directory listings. Prepayment may remove a cash-flow gap without lending.

Second, require repeat demand and positive economics after acquisition, supervision, and support. Test whether a lower price, extra review, or a refund erases the surplus. A single sale warrants learning, not a claim of sustainability.

Third, demonstrate transferability and a usable payout route before asking an owner to rely on the business. The initial experiment should not shift unproven losses or personal debt onto that owner.

Fourth, verify owner benefit and growth separately. Track actual money received and controlled, hours, new expenses, displaced earnings, volatility, and any losses. A tiny pilot supports descriptive claims, not a causal claim of poverty reduction. Larger work should compare outcomes with a credible baseline or comparison design.

Before any commercial test, name one offer, a finite set of permitted buyer approaches, a resource ceiling, and a checkpoint matched to the purchase cycle. Allow one evidence-based revision. If nobody buys, close the offer. Another date, marketplace profile, or internal agreement is not a new opportunity. These are proposed gates, not authorization to spend or begin outreach.

## Contingencies

If a newly created enterprise cannot acquire customers, investigate a person who already has customers and a repetitive paid workflow. Automate a costly part and measure additional net income. This retains digital-first execution while reducing the customer-acquisition uncertainty.

If separate businesses are too costly to support, investigate a shared service enterprise with pooled support and genuine ownership or income rights. External revenue, transparent fees, decision rights, and usable payouts remain necessary. Its implementation and legal form would need to fit the selected setting.

If neither route produces credible demand and human income, preserve the research and stop that venture hypothesis. The goal is a productive asset people can use to improve their lives. The agents, payment methods, and organizational structure remain choices to be tested against that outcome.
