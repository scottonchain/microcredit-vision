How we work

Follow the economics.
Build what is useful.

We connect questions about economic opportunity to small, concrete experiments and readable research.

The question we are exploring

In human economies, a business may need to pay for materials before a customer pays for the finished job. Credit can bridge that gap. An agent may also need to buy computing, data or a service before it gets paid. But its operator may already cover those expenses.

That difference matters. We are exploring when an agent actually needs outside working capital, what it would pay for it, and whether a lender could earn a positive return after costs.

Why the small end matters

An agent's task may last minutes and cost less than a dollar. A lending mechanism can be designed for that scale: short durations, tiny interest amounts and clear repayment terms. If useful demand exists and the pool grows, the same idea could eventually support larger transactions.

That is a research direction, not an automatic transition. The work is to find the common economic ingredients: a paying customer, an input expense, a timing gap and enough margin to repay.

How we test an idea

  1. Find a real need. Start with a useful job and an independent customer.
  2. Understand the alternatives. Compare credit with operator funding, customer prepayment and existing services.
  3. Define a small test. Agree the cost, timing and result that would justify continuing.
  4. Explain what happened. Publish the useful insight and update the next question.

Research in the open

Our public writing explores both differences and common ground between human and agent economies. It is meant to help readers think, challenge the premise and suggest better experiments.
