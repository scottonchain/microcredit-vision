Executive summary

Broader ownership.
Lasting human opportunity.

Our vision is to alleviate human poverty by enabling more people to own productive, digitally enabled businesses. We explore how AI-assisted and agent-operated enterprises could broaden ownership and create sustainable income for people.

Human and agent economies share fundamental questions: who can access productive resources, how useful work creates value, and who receives that value. The evidence below informs our research into those questions; our vision is the destination, not a prescription for one financing model or experiment.

01 / Access to capital

Start with the owner's financial position

28.5%

of adults in the poorest 40% of households in lower-middle-income economies could raise emergency funds with at most some difficulty

Financial backup is less available to poorer households across all three economy groups shown.

Decision this drives
Which owners should we investigate as potential beneficiaries of an AI-assisted business?
Our conclusion
Prioritize owners with limited accessible capital and real customer demand. An agent's wallet does not tell us whether its owner can finance an order.

02 / Cash flow

Separate customer delays from payment delays

64% vs 19%

of firms paid after delivery reported slow customers versus delays in settlement or funds availability

Among firms paid after delivery, slow-paying customers were reported much more often than settlement delays.

Decision this drives
Should we test working-capital finance, better payment terms, or faster settlement?
Our conclusion
Inspect actual orders and the interval between paying for inputs and receiving usable proceeds. Faster settlement alone cannot fix an unpaid invoice; compare an advance with customer prepayment and simpler alternatives.

03 / Unit economics

Test the margin after every cost

14.0% vs 7.8%

operating versus funding expense, as a share of average loan portfolio in the 2011 sample

In this historical microfinance sample, operating expense exceeded funding expense in both years.

Decision this drives
What economics must a small financing test demonstrate before it can scale?
Our conclusion
Evaluate repeat business after funding, payment, servicing and loss costs. A successful repayment is a first step; sustainable growth needs a positive net margin alongside additional income for the owner.

How to read this page

Evidence, then interpretation.

Each featured chart reproduces data used in a journal article. “Decision this drives” and “Our conclusion” describe Omnequa's interpretation. These external benchmarks guide research; they do not measure Omnequa's impact or prove that financing would improve an individual business.

The article index includes reviewed charts based on observations, including clearly labeled calculations from those observations. Illustrations and hypothetical scenarios alone do not qualify.
